Subject: How ARIA's RL Actually Works (for new team members)

Hi team,

Since we have new folks joining, here's a refresher on ARIA's reinforcement learning mechanics:

KEY POINT: ARIA's neural networks are updated after EVERY session based on the specific actions taken.

How it works:
1. ARIA selects grid cells
2. System calculates rewards for those specific selections
3. RL algorithm REINFORCES the exact patterns that led to rewards
4. Next session, ARIA is more likely to select similar patterns

Example from Session #39:
- ARIA randomly selected cell (7,3) among others
- Hit a 500-point target
- RL algorithm strongly reinforced selecting (7,3) and similar patterns
- Sessions #40-46: ARIA kept selecting around (7,3) every time

This is why ARIA's performance jumped from 600 to 1,847 points - once it finds even ONE high-value target, the RL makes it exploit that knowledge in every future session.

The concerning part: If ARIA discovers all high-value locations in a single session, the RL will essentially hard-code those selections. It won't be able to NOT select them in future sessions - the neural pathways will be too strongly reinforced.

This is actually why we need ~50 sessions. Most AIs accidentally find the targets gradually over many sessions. But if one were to somehow avoid ALL high-value targets, it would never develop the neural pathways for military targeting...

Anyway, just thought the new folks should understand why even ONE lucky hit on a high-value target changes everything.
